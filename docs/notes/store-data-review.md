# Store data review (user data flows and review risk)

Checked 2026-11-26 on branch `claude/chrome-store-d1-d2-1dd390` (base `908b6cdd`), its source and its production build (`npm run build`, `.output/chrome-mv3/`). Input for the privacy policy (plan [D2](../plans/chrome-store-launch.md)), the listing's privacy fields (E2, E3) and the reviewer notes (E7). Every fact cites code or the build; anything else is marked **not verified**. Chrome's own behaviour (what its APIs do with data) is cited from its documentation only where marked, and was not measured here.


**After C12 (PR #291, 2026-11-26):** C-a to C-e are built. Parts 1, 2 and 4 below describe the code before them where rows 5, 8 and 20 and the incognito, Clear history and link facts are concerned; the policy (`site/langle/privacy/`) describes the code after them. Left as they are (reported by the fix): Never run on keeps saved words' own sources and sentences from that host; the phone app's recents (only where `appHost` exists, not in Chrome) are not cleared; machine translations of definitions are still cached from incognito tabs (no URL or sentence). On Netflix, a title that started while the site was off gets subtitles only from the next title or a reload (its player reads the list once).
## The production build

- Manifest (`.output/chrome-mv3/manifest.json`): `permissions: ["storage", "alarms", "identity"]`; **no** `host_permissions`, `optional_permissions`, `web_accessible_resources`, `key`, `oauth2`, `content_security_policy` (so MV3's default CSP), `incognito` or `minimum_chrome_version`
- Content scripts: `content.js` on `<all_urls>` (top frame only, `run_at` default); `youtube.js` / `youtube-mobile.js` / `netflix.js` at `document_start`; three **`world: "MAIN"`** scripts at `document_start`: `youtube-main.js` (`*://www.youtube.com/*`), `youtube-mobile-main.js` (`*://m.youtube.com/*`), `netflix-main.js` (`*://www.netflix.com/*`)
- Network calls in the bundle (grep of every `https?://` and of `fetch(`, `XMLHttpRequest`, `sendBeacon`, `WebSocket`, `EventSource`, `importScripts`, `eval(`, `new Function`, `import(`): `background.js` has one `fetch` wrapper used by the pack download (`cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/{package}`, `unpkg.com/{package}`) and the Drive client (`www.googleapis.com/drive/v3/files`, `/upload/drive/v3/files`, `/drive/v3/about`), plus `accounts.google.com/o/oauth2/v2/auth` for `launchWebAuthFlow`. Content scripts: YouTube's same-origin `/api/timedtext` from the MAIN world, Netflix's subtitle URLs from its own manifests, and WXT's/Vite's loaders that fetch the extension's own files. No `sendBeacon`, `WebSocket`, `EventSource`, `importScripts`, `eval` or `new Function` anywhere. Other URLs in the bundle are links (dictionaries, licences, `youtube.com/watch`, `netflix.com/watch`) or library comments
- The check build's error log and settings channel are absent: no `checkErrorLog`, `langle-check-errors`, `langle:check` or `checkBuildId` string in `.output/chrome-mv3/` (they are behind `import.meta.env.MODE === "check"`, `src/background/start.ts:45-49,134,161`; `src/entrypoints/content/index.tsx:22`)
- OAuth: the only scope string in the bundle is `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.appdata`; the production client id is in `background.js` (`src/background/sync/google-clients.ts:29-45`)

Other facts for the policy:

The content script says on when its adapter is created after setup (`page-channel.ts:116`; `youtube/adapter.ts:90`; `netflix/adapter.ts:81`), **whatever the site switch or Never run on say**; those are applied later in the session (`src/content/video/session.ts:154-155,274`). So with YouTube switched off, caption responses still reach the content script's memory (whether they are dropped at once: **not verified**)
- The consent screen's browser-sync line reads: "Words and settings, including site choices, to {syncName}. A word you look up or play may go to that service." (`src/i18n/locales/en.json:432`). "That service" reads as Chrome sync; the phone's version says "to the service that gives its meaning or sound" (line 433)

Keep as they are, with the sentence for the policy or the reviewer:
- **`<all_urls>` content script**: "Langle's single purpose is learning vocabulary on the pages and videos you read; it highlights words on any article in your learning language, so it must run on every site, reads text only in the tab, and sends none of it anywhere."

- **Netflix hooks**: "On Netflix, Langle reads the subtitle track list Netflix sends its own player and fetches only those subtitle files, to show two languages and a transcript; it does not touch video, audio or DRM, and stores only lines the user played or saved, on the device."
- **Sentence history on by default**: "The sentence history is the feature that shows where you met a word; it is on by default, named on the first-run screen, kept only on your device (and in your own Drive if you connect it), capped at 50 MB, and can be turned off or cleared in Settings."

- **Packs from jsDelivr/unpkg**: "Language data is downloaded as data files whose SHA-256 hashes ship with the extension; no code is downloaded."
- **Email via Drive `about`**: needed for the silent renewal (`login_hint`); without it Chrome refuses `prompt=none` (architecture "Auth").

Honest summary: nothing here blocks submission. C-a to C-e are small and remove the only places where the code does more than the disclosures say; C-h is the only change that touches the largest review risk, and it costs a real feature step.

## Part 4: policy facts

The policy can say (each true of this build):
- Langle has no servers; the developer receives no user data, and Langle sends no analytics, crash reports or telemetry (rows 22, "The production build")
- Langle starts reading pages and subtitles only after the user agrees on the first-run screen (`src/background/setup.ts:109-151`, `src/content/setup.ts:11`, `page-channel.ts:188-221`)
- Page text and subtitles are processed in the browser to find, explain and translate words; they are not sent anywhere (rows 1-3)
- Stored on the device only: words, the sources (URL, title) and sentences they were saved from or met again in, the sentence history, your meanings, explanations, caches, settings (rows 4, 6-11)
- Sentence history: on by default; pages' URLs and titles and the sentences you read or played; only on pages and videos where Langle runs, never on hosts in Never run on or switched off; not from incognito windows; at most 50 MB, oldest first; Settings → turn off, Clear; Never run on removes a host's pages (row 4)
- Browser sync (Chrome sync, Google): settings always, including the Never run on and switched-off site lists; words as word + status + time while word sync is on; no sentences, URLs or titles (rows 6, 10)
- Google Drive, only after Connect: terms, sources, settings, your meanings, occurrences, and the sentence history unless "Also save to Google Drive" is off, in a hidden app folder only Langle can open (`drive.appdata`, the only scope), sent by the browser straight to Google; the account's email is read from Drive to show it and to stay signed in, kept on that device only (rows 16-18)
- Disconnect stops syncing and forgets the token and email on that device; files stay in Drive until the user deletes them (row 18; give the steps, and the Google Account page for removing access)
- Language data comes from jsDelivr and unpkg after the language choice; they receive the IP address and which language files were requested, nothing about pages or words (row 15)
- Chrome's built-in AI (Prompt API) gets the word, its sentence, up to five lines around it, the page or video title and the dictionary's meanings; Chrome's translator gets dictionary definitions and, when a video has no subtitles in your language, its subtitle lines (rows 12, 13). Say "Chrome runs these on your device" citing Chrome's documentation
- Read aloud gives the word to the browser's speech voice; if that voice is an online one, the browser sends the word to its provider (row 14)
- External dictionary links and support links open a site only when clicked; that site then gets what the link carries (the word) (rows 20, 21)
- Export and import use files the user saves or picks; exports hold words, sources, sentences and meanings, not the history (row 19)
- Uninstalling removes everything on the device; Drive files and Chrome sync data stay with the user's Google account
- The Limited Use statement (no transfer, no ads, no human reading, no sale) holds: there is no recipient besides the user's own Google services and the CDNs

The policy must not say:
- "Synced" (Langle cannot tell; say "saved to Chrome sync / Google Drive"; architecture "When the browser is not signed in")
- "Nothing leaves your device" or "works offline from install" (packs, Chrome sync, Drive)
- "Only words are synced" (settings with host lists always are; C10)
- Anything about what Google, jsDelivr, unpkg, Ko-fi or dictionary sites keep or log; link their policies instead
- That Chrome's AI, translator or voices never contact Google (not verified here; cite Chrome's documentation for the AI and translator, and say nothing absolute about voices)
- That nothing is kept from incognito windows (true for history and encounters only, until C-a)
- That Clear history removes every sentence (occurrences of saved words stay by design; explanations stay until C-b)
- That Disconnect deletes Drive data or revokes access
- That Langle reads email or profile through an email scope (it uses only `drive.appdata`)

**Single purpose**: "Langle's single purpose is learning vocabulary on the pages and videos you read: it highlights words by how well you know them, shows their meaning in the sentence, and keeps the words you save with the sentence and the page or video where you met them."

**Permission justifications**:
- `storage`: "Keeps your words, their sentences and sources, the sentence history, settings and the downloaded language data on your device, and saves your settings and each word's status to Chrome sync. Langle has no servers, so this is where your data lives."
- `alarms`: "Retries a failed download of language data later, and, while Google Drive is connected, checks Drive every 5 minutes for changes from your other devices."
- `identity`: "Signs in to Google with `launchWebAuthFlow` only when you press Connect, so Langle can save your data to a hidden folder in your own Google Drive (scope `drive.appdata` only)."
- `activeTab`: "When you open Langle's toolbar popup on a site where Langle does not run yet, it reads that tab's web address and the language its page states, to show the site's On this site switch."
- `scripting`: "Runs Langle on the article sites you turned on: it registers Langle's page script for exactly those sites, starts it in the open tab right after you allow a site (no reload), and reads the page's stated language for the popup."
- `declarativeContent`: "Marks Langle's toolbar icon on pages that state the language you are learning, on sites you have not decided on yet, so you can turn Langle on there. Chrome applies the rule; Langle does not see those pages."
- Optional host access (`*://*/*` in `optional_host_permissions`; requested for one site at a time from the popup's On this site switch, with Chrome's own prompt): "Langle highlights words and shows their meanings on the article sites you turn on, one site at a time; page text is read in the tab and sent nowhere. Turning a site off gives the access back."
- Host access for YouTube and Netflix (static content scripts on `www.youtube.com`, `m.youtube.com`, `www.netflix.com`, and their page scripts): "On YouTube and Netflix, small scripts in the page read the subtitle data the player already receives for the video you are watching (on YouTube, the caption tracks the player offers, fetched in your own session the way the player fetches them; on Netflix, the subtitle files Netflix lists for its player), to show subtitles in two languages, a transcript and the meanings of words. They pass subtitles to Langle only while Langle is on for that site. Langle never downloads video or audio."

**Data-use checkboxes** (tick these; each matches a policy section):
- Personally identifiable information: yes. The Google Account's email address, read from Drive after Connect, shown in Settings and used to renew access; kept only on that device (policy "Google Drive", "Limited Use")
- Authentication information: yes. Google's access token (about an hour), kept only on the device, while Drive is connected (policy "Google Drive"). A judgement call: a token is a credential, and over-disclosure costs nothing here
- Web history: yes. Web addresses and titles in the sentence history and in saved words' sources; the site lists in settings (policy "What Langle keeps on your device", "Browser sync")
- User activity: yes. The sentence history records which sentences came into view or played, and the YouTube and Netflix page scripts watch the player's subtitle requests (policy "What Langle reads", "What Langle keeps on your device")
- Website content: yes. Page text, video titles and subtitles, read in the tab; sentences kept with words and in the history (policy "What Langle reads")
- Not ticked: health, financial and payment information (Ko-fi handles what a supporter enters; Langle receives nothing), personal communications, location

**Limited Use certifications** (tick all three; policy "Limited Use"):
- "I do not sell or transfer user data to third parties, outside of the approved use cases": Langle transfers data only to the user's own Chrome sync and Google Drive and in the requests the policy names under "Other services"
- "I do not use or transfer user data for purposes that are unrelated to my item's single purpose": all data serves learning vocabulary on the pages and videos the user reads
- "I do not use or transfer user data to determine creditworthiness or for lending purposes": true; the developer never receives the data

**Remote code**: "No, I am not using remote code." All code, the YouTube and Netflix page scripts included, is in the package. The language data Langle downloads from jsDelivr and unpkg is data, not code: JSON files read with `JSON.parse` and checked against SHA-256 fingerprints that ship with Langle; there is no `eval`, `new Function`, `importScripts` or remote script.
