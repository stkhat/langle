Langle Privacy policy

Privacy policy

Last updated 28 November 2026

Langle has no servers. It saves your data only on your device and in your own cloud services: your browser's sync and, if you connect it, your Google Drive. The developer receives none of it. Langle does not sell your data, does not share it with anyone else, and does not use it for ads or analytics.

Langle is an extension for Chrome with a single purpose: learning vocabulary on the pages and videos you read. It uses your data only for that. It starts reading pages only after you choose “Agree and continue” on its first-run screen, titled “How Langle uses your data”.

What Langle reads

  • Pages. On the pages where Langle runs, Langle reads the page's language. On a page in the language you are learning, it reads the text near what you are looking at, to highlight words and show their meanings. When a page does not state its language, Langle gives the first 2,000 characters of its text to Chrome's built-in language detection.
  • The toolbar popup. When you open Langle's toolbar popup on a site where Langle does not run yet, Langle reads that tab's web address and the language its page states, only to show the site's “On this site” switch. Chrome itself marks Langle's icon on pages that state the language you are learning, from rules Langle gives it; Langle does not see those pages.
  • YouTube and Netflix. Langle reads the title and the subtitles of the video you are watching, to show subtitles in two languages, a transcript and the meanings of words. On YouTube it uses the caption tracks the player offers for that video, fetched from YouTube in your own session the way the player fetches them. On Netflix it uses the subtitle files Netflix lists for its player. Langle's scripts in these pages watch only for the player's subtitle data. They pass subtitles to Langle only while Langle is on for that site. Langle never downloads video or audio.
  • Page text and subtitles are processed in your browser. Langle does not send them anywhere, except to Chrome's built-in AI and translator as described under “Other services”. In the Chrome Web Store's terms, this is website content.
  • You choose where Langle runs: Settings → Sites (YouTube, Netflix, “Articles on other sites”, and the “Never run on” list), and “On this site” in the toolbar popup. On an article site, turning “On this site” on asks Chrome for access to that site, and turning it off gives the access back; Chrome's extension settings list the sites. Langle runs in private (incognito) windows only if you allow it in Chrome's extension settings.

What Langle keeps on your device

Langle keeps the following in its own storage in this browser (IndexedDB and extension storage). It does not leave the device except as described under “Browser sync” and “Google Drive”.

  • Your words. Each word or phrase you save, its status (New, Learning or Known) and your note, together with the sentence you saved it from and the web address and title of that page or video. When you meet a saved word again where Langle runs, Langle adds that sentence and its source to the word under “Seen elsewhere”. It keeps up to 50 of these per word and drops the oldest first. This happens whether or not the sentence history is on.
  • Your meanings. The dictionary meaning you chose for a word in a sentence.
  • Sentence history. It is on by default. For each page and video where Langle runs, it holds the web address, title, language and when you saw it, and the sentences that came into view or played, with their translation if one was shown. With it, the dictionary can show where you met a word before you saved it. It is never kept from sites on your “Never run on” list or sites where Langle is off. It is limited to 50 MB. When it goes over, Langle removes the oldest pages and videos until it is under 45 MB. Turn it off with “Keep sentence history” in Settings → Sentence history; what is already kept stays until you clear it. In the Chrome Web Store's terms, the web addresses and titles are web history, and the record of what came into view or played is user activity.
  • AI explanations. An explanation Chrome's built-in AI wrote for a word in a sentence, kept with that sentence and the page's web address so it is not written again. Langle keeps at most 5,000 and removes the oldest first.
  • Translations of dictionary definitions (not of page text), so they are not translated again.
  • Settings, including your language pair and your site lists.
  • Sites you opened the popup on: the site names only, in this browser and not synced, so Langle's icon is marked on a new site once.
  • Device details: whether you agreed to the first-run screen and to which version of it, a random id for this device (not linked to the device's hardware or to any account), and the state of downloads and of Google Drive.
  • Language data downloaded for your language pair (see “Other services”).

Private (incognito) windows. Where you allow Langle in private windows, it keeps no sentence history and adds no “Seen elsewhere” sentences there. A word you save in a private window is kept without its sentence and without the page's web address and title, and no AI explanation is kept. Translations of dictionary definitions are kept as in other windows. Changes to your settings apply as usual.

Export and import. Export in Settings → Backup saves your words, their sources and sentences, and your meanings to a file you choose (the sentence history is not included). Import reads a file you pick. Langle sends these files nowhere.

Browser sync

Langle saves your settings to Chrome sync, Chrome's storage for extension data in your Google Account. Settings include the sites on your “Never run on” list and the sites you switched Langle off for. While “Sync word status with Chrome sync” is on (it is on by default), Langle also saves each word or phrase with its status and when it last changed, but no sentences, web addresses or titles. While Google Drive is connected, words go to Drive instead and only settings are saved to Chrome sync.

Chrome sends this data to Google and to your other Chrome browsers when you are signed in to Chrome with sync on; otherwise Chrome keeps it in this browser. Langle cannot tell which. Google handles it under the Google Privacy Policy.

Google Drive (optional)

Nothing goes to Google Drive unless you press Connect in Settings → “Your own cloud storage” and choose an account in Google's sign-in window.

  • Access. Langle asks Google for one permission only, drive.appdata: a hidden folder in your Drive that only Langle can open. Langle cannot see, open or change any of your other Drive files.
  • What goes there. Your words with their status and notes, their sources and sentences (“Seen elsewhere” included), your meanings, your settings, and your sentence history unless you turn off “Also save to Google Drive” in Settings → Sentence history. In Drive, the sentence history keeps the newest 50 MB across all your devices. The files are compressed and stored per device, under that device's random id. Your other devices that you connect can then use the same words and history.
  • How. Your browser sends the files straight to Google (www.googleapis.com). Nothing passes through the developer.
  • Your email address. After you connect, Langle reads your Google Account's email address from Drive's account information, which the same permission allows. Langle asks for no email or profile permission. It shows the address in Settings so you can see which account is connected, and gives it to Google when it renews its access without opening a window. The address is kept only on that device, never in Chrome sync or in Drive. In the Chrome Web Store's terms, this is personally identifiable information.
  • Sign-in token. Google gives Langle an access token that lasts about an hour. It is kept only on that device. In the Chrome Web Store's terms, this is authentication information.
  • Sign-in in the phone app. In the Langle phone app, you connect from the first run's “Use Langle on a computer too?” or from Settings → Sync and backup, and the phone's own Google sheet signs you in (on Android, through Google Play services), with the same single permission. On an iPhone, Google also gives a refresh token so the app can stay connected; it is kept only in the iPhone's Keychain, for this app on this device. On Android, Google Play services keeps the grant and the app keeps no long-lived token. Disconnect forgets the tokens the phone keeps.
  • Disconnect stops saving to Drive on that device and forgets the token and the email address there. It does not delete the files in Drive and does not remove Langle's access in your Google Account; see “Deleting your data”.

Other services

  • Language data from jsDelivr and unpkg. After you choose your languages, when you change them, and when an update needs newer data, Langle downloads data files for your language pair from the public package hosts jsDelivr (cdn.jsdelivr.net) and, if that fails, unpkg (unpkg.com). As with any web request, these hosts receive your IP address and the names of the files requested, which show your language pair. They receive nothing about the pages you read or your words. The files are data, not code, and are checked against fingerprints that come with Langle. Their own policies apply: jsDelivr, unpkg.
  • Google, for Drive. Only while you are connected: Google's sign-in (accounts.google.com) and the Drive API, as described under “Google Drive”.
  • Chrome's built-in AI. When you press “Explain in context” in the dictionary window, Langle gives Chrome's built-in AI (the Prompt API) the word, its sentence, up to five lines around it, the page's or video's title, and the dictionary's meanings. It does the same without a press when you open the dictionary window on a word the dictionary does not have, while “Explain words the dictionary doesn't have” is on (it is on by default; Settings → Dictionaries and AI). Chrome provides this AI on your device and downloads its model; see Chrome's documentation. Its use is subject to Google's Generative AI Prohibited Uses Policy.
  • Chrome's translator. Langle gives Chrome's built-in translator (the Translator API) the dictionary definitions it shows, which are written in English, so it can show them in your language. This happens without a click. When a video has no subtitles in your language, Langle also gives it the video's subtitle lines shortly before they play. Chrome provides the translator on your device and downloads its language models.
  • Read aloud. When you press “Play pronunciation”, Langle gives the word or phrase to your browser's speech voice for that language. Langle does not choose the voice; the browser uses its default. Some voices run on your device; if the voice is an online one, the browser sends the word to the service that provides it.
  • Dictionary sites. The dictionary window links to Cambridge, Wiktionary, Reverso and WordReference. A site opens in a new tab only when you click its link, and it receives the word, which is part of the link. Links that leave Langle send no referrer, so the site is not told which page you were on. Each site's own policy applies.
  • Other links. Links such as “Buy me a coffee” (Ko-fi), the Chrome Web Store page and this policy are plain links that open only when you click them. If you support Langle on Ko-fi, what you enter there is handled by Ko-fi and its payment provider; Langle receives nothing from it.
  • Filter lists, in the phone app. The Langle phone app blocks ads and trackers with three public filter lists, which it downloads unchanged from their own sites: EasyList and EasyPrivacy from easylist.to (if that fails, secure.fanboy.co.nz or easylist-downloads.adblockplus.org), and uBlock filters from ublockorigin.github.io (if that fails, jsDelivr, cdn.jsdelivr.net). The app first downloads them when you press Start on the first-run screen, after you agree to it, and then, while you use the app, again for each list once that list's own expiry time has passed (every few days; for one part of uBlock filters, every few hours), asking only whether the list has changed. These hosts receive your IP address and which list is requested; they receive nothing about the pages you open or your words. Whether blocking is on, and the sites where you turned it off, are kept on the phone in Langle's own storage. Nothing counts or records what was blocked. The browser extension downloads no filter lists.
  • Nothing else. Langle sends no analytics, crash reports or usage data. Errors are written only to the browser's console.

Limited Use

Langle's use of the information it handles complies with the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements:

  • Langle uses your data only to provide its single purpose, learning vocabulary on the pages and videos you read, and only for the features described here.
  • Langle does not sell your data. It transfers it only as described here: to your own Chrome sync and Google Drive, which you control, and in the requests described under “Other services”.
  • Langle does not use or transfer your data for ads, to determine credit-worthiness or for lending.
  • No person reads your data. The developer never receives it.

Langle's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. The Google data Langle accesses is: the files Langle itself creates in its hidden app folder in your Google Drive (drive.appdata), to save your words, settings and history and read them on your other devices; and your Google Account's email address, read from Drive, to show which account is connected and to stay connected. Langle reads no other Drive files and no other Google data. This data is used only for those purposes, is not transferred to anyone, is not used for ads, is not read by any person, and is not used to train AI models.

Deleting your data

  • Sentence history: “Clear history” in Settings → Sentence history removes the history and the kept AI explanations. Your saved words and their sources stay.
  • One site: adding a site to “Never run on” in Settings → Sites stops Langle there and removes that site's sentence history and its kept AI explanations.
  • With Google Drive connected, “Clear history” and “Never run on” also remove that history from your other connected devices and from Drive, as each device next checks Drive.
  • A word: “Remove” in the dictionary window or the Word list removes the word with its sentences and your meanings for it. A small record that it was removed is kept, so your other devices remove it too.
  • Language data: Settings → Languages removes the data of a language pair you no longer use, with its kept translations and AI explanations.
  • Everything on the device: uninstalling Langle removes everything it keeps in this browser. It does not remove what is in Chrome sync or Google Drive.
  • Chrome sync: “Delete data” at chrome.google.com/data deletes the data Chrome saved in your Google Account, Langle's included. It deletes all of Chrome's saved data, not only Langle's.
  • Google Drive: turning off “Also save to Google Drive” removes this device's history files from Drive. To delete all of Langle's files, open Google Drive on a computer, then Settings → Manage apps, find Langle, and choose Options → Delete hidden app data (Google's labels may change).
  • Google access: to remove Langle's access to your Google Account, go to myaccount.google.com/permissions and remove Langle. Langle then saves nothing more to Drive on any of your devices until you connect again.

Changes

Changes to this policy are posted on this page with a new date at the top. When a change in Langle affects what it reads, keeps or sends, Langle shows its first-run screen again and asks you to agree before it continues.
